Missing residues

4A1X: see where the model has holes. Gaps in the chain and missing ends are listed in the tree and drawn in the 3D view, and a gap can be rebuilt if you choose to.

Residues without electron density are not in the model, and the file lists them (its REMARK 465 records). ADapt compares each chain's sequence with the residues present and lists what is missing under missing residues:

  • gap: SER-3--GKGG--PRO2: GKGG is missing between SER -3 and PRO 2;
  • N term: GEL and C term: GSHHHHHH: residues missing at the start or end of the chain (here the His tag).

By default missing residues are information only: nothing is rebuilt, and the PDBQT files are the same whether or not a chain has any. A gap near the pocket is a reason to prefer another assembly or entry, or to rebuild it (below) knowing what that gives you.

A stretch whose ends meet is not shown. If the residues on either side of a missing stretch are within 3.5 Å of each other (C to N), ADFR treats the chain as continuous there and does not report it. For example, 1JFF chain A lists residues 35 to 60 as missing, but GLY 34 and HIS 61 are 2.68 Å apart.

Rebuilding a gap

Each gap between two residues of a chain has a rebuild box in its params column. Nothing is rebuilt until you tick one, and with no box ticked every file ADapt writes is exactly what it would be without this feature. Ticking a box builds the gap's residues at once and draws them with magenta carbons, labelled rebuilt: GKGG; the gap's orange marker is hidden while they are drawn. What is drawn is what the receptor file will contain: the same coordinates. Unticking removes them.

How the residues are made:

  1. Their names and numbers come from the file's REMARK 465 records. SEQRES gives one letter per residue, which cannot tell a phosphoserine (SEP) from a serine, and the numbers that follow the residue before a gap are not always the deposited ones (1A3R chain H jumps from CYS 128 to GLY 131).
  2. PDBFixer places their heavy atoms on an arc between the two residues they join, with the residues near the gap around them, and relaxes them in its own soft force field. It is asked for the chosen gaps only: it adds no other atom, so side chains stay ADFR's rotamers and your choices.
  3. PDBFixer's soft force field leaves a sketch, not a model: on ADFR's test entries its loops had peptide bonds anywhere from cis to trans, consecutive CA atoms 1.4 to 4.1 Å apart, residues of the wrong hand (D) and threonines in their allo form. So each new side chain is first turned about its CA until CB sits where an L residue's would, an inverted Thr or Ile CB is righted, and the residues are then refined in Amber ff14SB (OpenMM): every observed atom held fixed, after a short stage in which a soft repulsion lets the loop untangle. Every new peptide bond is kept trans and every new CA L by restraints with flat wells, which exert no force on a loop inside them. Charges are off: the aim is sane covalent geometry without clashes, not a model of the loop's electrostatics.
  4. The loop is checked against the tolerances below. If it is outside them the build is made again with the next seed (1, then 2, then 3), and the first loop that passes is kept. If none does, nothing is written: the box goes back and the tree says why.
  5. When the receptor is written, reduce adds the hydrogens of the new residues along with everyone else's, and they get Gasteiger charges and AutoDock types like the rest. They are written in chain order, between the residues they join.

When a gap cannot be rebuilt

The box is replaced by the reason, under the gap's entry:

A gap is not offered when:

  • REMARK 465 does not list exactly its residues, or numbers one of them with a number the chain already uses (a water or a hetero group of the same chain, for example);
  • one of its residues is not one of the 20 amino acids. A selenomethionine (MSE) is the exception: it is built as the methionine ADFR writes for every MSE;
  • a residue either side is not one of the 20 amino acids (after MSE becomes MET), lacks its N, CA or C, or the residue before ends with an OXT;
  • its ends are further apart than its residues can span: CA to CA more than 3.8 Å per peptide bond (n + 1 bonds for n residues);
  • the loop built for it fails the checks above, as 4A1X's GK does: this is found by building it, so the reason appears after you tick the box.

Missing ends are not offered. Only one end of a missing terminus is held, so where its residues go would be a guess.

In an assembly

A gap is chosen for a chain, in every tab that has the chain. In an assembly built by symmetry each copy of the chain gets the gap rebuilt in its own place, among its own neighbours, as ADFR completes side chains in every copy. The REMARK line is written once per gap, and session.json records each copy. Copies are drawn translucent, as the rest of them is.

The new residues are in the receptor when the residues on both sides of the gap are, and never in a ligand. A gap in a chain written as a ligand is not filled.

A rebuilt loop is one model. It is built without any knowledge of the binding site or of the ligand you will dock, from one seed, and it is one of many conformations the loop could take; in the crystal it had none, which is why it is missing. It gives the receptor a chain without holes and keeps the docking box from seeing an opening that is not there, but it is not evidence of where the loop is. A missing loop near the site is still a reason to choose another copy of the chain, another assembly or another entry.

Reproducible

Everything random in a build is seeded (the seed kept is in the REMARK line) and runs on OpenMM's Reference platform, so the same input gives the same coordinates on any machine and in any session; ADapt's tests check this. The surroundings a loop is built among include every location of every atom, so choosing another alternate location does not move it. The side chains ADFR completes next to a loop are built on the default alternate location, which is the same in every run (most atoms, then highest occupancy, then first letter; see alternate locations).

A build takes a few seconds per gap (3 to 12 s on ADFR's test entries): PDBFixer's own relaxation, then the refinement, once per seed tried. The first build after ADapt starts also loads OpenMM and PDBFixer, which adds a few seconds.

AGFRgui never showed missing residues, because ADFR's detection had a defect, corrected in ADFR 0.0.23; see ADapt and AGFRgui. Rebuilding gaps is ADapt's own; AGFRgui and ADFR's command line have no equivalent.
