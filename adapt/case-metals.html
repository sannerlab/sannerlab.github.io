Metals, ions and unsupported atoms

Give an ion the charge AutoDock needs; know which atoms AutoDock cannot type, and replace an ion it cannot type by one it can.

Manual charges

AutoDock 4.1 has parameters for zinc, magnesium, calcium, iron and manganese, but the Gasteiger model that charges the rest of the receptor cannot charge them, nor a lone halide ion. Single-atom residues of zinc, magnesium, calcium, iron, manganese, chlorine, bromine and iodine are therefore listed under manual charges, with the formal charge that will be written in the params column. They are not in the receptor by default. Check one when it matters for your pocket, for instance a catalytic zinc.

The default charge is the one the wwPDB Chemical Component Dictionary gives the residue name, which tells the oxidation state apart where the element alone does not:

Residue nameIonDefault charge
ZN, MG, CAZn²⁺, Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺+2
FE2, MNiron(II), manganese(II)+2
FE, MN3iron(III), manganese(III)+3
CL, BR, IODchloride, bromide, iodide−1

A residue name the dictionary does not define as a one-atom ion gets its element's usual charge (+2 for the metals, −1 for the halogens). An edited charge is written to the receptor file, to every symmetry copy of the ion, and to the session record, and the receptor's header gives it (REMARK 850 AGFR custom charge for A:ZN:401: = 2.0000).

Unsupported atom types

A residue with an atom outside the AutoDock 4.1 atom types is listed under unknown atom types, with the offending atoms beneath it, and it is left out of the receptor whatever its category, so a cofactor such as vitamin B12, with its cobalt, starts outside too. These entries have no checkboxes: AutoDock has no parameters for such an atom. If a residue with an unsupported atom is put in the receptor anyway (with agfr --heteroToKeep, for instance), it is dropped when the file is written, and the header says so (REMARK 850 AGFR left out A:B12:101:_ (atoms of type CO)).

Replacing an ion

Far from the pocket, leaving such an atom out is harmless. A metal ion near the pocket, though, shapes what the ligand meets. Right-click a single ion under unknown atom types to replace it by an ion AutoDock types: Zn, Mg, Ca, Fe or Mn, at the same position. The replacement takes the original ion's formal charge from the Chemical Component Dictionary (+2 for CU), which you can edit like any manual charge.

The receptor file has a zinc atom where the copper was, and its header records the replacement (REMARK 850 AGFR substituted A:CU:100:_ CU -> ZN charge 2.00). The same menu on the replaced ion offers Undo the replacement. Whether a zinc is a fair stand-in for a copper in your pocket is a chemical judgement; the replacement only makes the choice possible and records it. On the command line: agfr --substitute "A:CU:100::ZN".
